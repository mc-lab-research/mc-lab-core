This is the first operational Research Case under the SemTL Architectural

materialization boundary and calibrate the research method, not to prove broad

## Why this case exists

### Question

Can one single-pass local labelled-transition consumer remain source-unchanged
when the same finite labelled transition relation is exposed through stored
stable witnesses and generated ephemeral witnesses?

### Decision enabled

Determine whether the local transition-relation contract should:

- retain its current minimum `input_range` posture;
- or require stable stored transition witnesses.

Whether persistent transition identity should be a separate capability is a
follow-on question. This case deliberately does not claim to discriminate it.

### Repository context

The current architecture already intends to support stored and generated
transition witnesses:

- [`TransitionRelation`](../../../include/mc_lab/semantic/facet/transition_relation.hpp)
  accepts an `input_range` and checks `target` against the range's actual
  reference, value, or proxy form;
- [`structural-facets.md`](../../semantic/structural-facets.md) states that a
  witness may be stored by reference, generated by value, proxied, or
  single-pass;
- [`explicit_system` and `lazy_system`](../../../tests/semantic/support/facet_models.hpp)
  provide stored and generated development fixtures;
- [`facet_concepts_test.cpp`](../../../tests/semantic/facet_concepts_test.cpp)
  confirms both fixtures satisfy the current concept.

This is exploratory design evidence, not a controlled result. The current
`lazy_system` uses `std::views::iota`, which does not pressure the architecture
with a genuinely single-pass range, ephemeral proxy lifetime, or an unchanged
semantic consumer shared with the stored model.

### Strategic relevance

The case is an initial test of materialization genericity and of whether the
Lean Research OS can convert architectural uncertainty into a bounded decision
without unnecessary process overhead.

### Scope

Included:

- finite labelled transition systems;
- local outgoing-transition enumeration from a supplied state;
- one stored borrowed representation;
- one genuinely single-pass generated representation;
- observation of transition label and target;
- a source state already known by the query;
- C++20 as the language baseline.

### Out of scope

- formalism genericity;
- global exploration;
- persistent predecessor maps or counterexample traces;
- acceptance;
- composition;
- mutation during enumeration;
- probabilistic, timed, weighted, or symbolic semantics;
- runtime plugin boundaries;
- and a universal claim about transition identity.

### Affected properties

- **P1 Representation independence** - can one semantic consumer tolerate two
  materially different witness representations without canonicalization?
- **P3 Algorithmic reuse** - does one consumer remain unchanged?
- **P6 Minimal structural assumptions** - does local enumeration require only
  single-pass access?

## Competing hypotheses

### H-A - Minimal semantic successor capability

**Statement:** A transition relation can expose local transition witnesses to a
consumer without requiring stored transition objects.

**Mechanism:** The consumer makes one pass over outgoing witnesses and observes
their label and target while each witness is valid. Persistent occurrence
identity is not part of this consumer's semantic requirement.

**Expected supporting observation:**

- consumer source remains unchanged;
- both representations meet the same minimum semantic requirements;
- the lazy model does not retain or materialize a complete transition
  collection;
- normalized semantic observations agree;
- integration needs only the model-local adaptations permitted by the freeze.

**Expected contradicting observation:**

- the consumer requires stable references, multiple passes, `size()`, or
  random access;
- a witness must survive iterator advancement;
- representation-specific branching appears;
- or the lazy subject must cache all outgoing transitions.

### H-B - Stable materialization is intrinsic to the current contract

**Statement:** The current relation or consumer design implicitly depends on
stable materialized transition witnesses.

**Mechanism:** Associated types, range handling, label access, target access, or
descriptor lifetime assumptions require witnesses that persist independently
of iteration.

**Expected supporting observation:**

- lazy conformance requires persistent storage;
- the consumer or CPO surface must change;
- the concept must require a `forward_range`, borrowed range, or another
  stronger property;
- or a stable descriptor leaks into the consumer.

**Expected contradicting observation:** The single-pass model works through
local adaptation without caching and without consumer changes.

## Experimental subjects

### SUBJECT-EXPLICIT-LTS-01

**Type:** MODEL and MATERIALIZATION

**Role:** DEVELOPMENT and CALIBRATION

**Formalism:** Finite labelled transition system.

**Representation:** States and transition records are stored. Outgoing
transitions are exposed through a borrowed `std::span`. A witness is a stable
reference for the model's lifetime.

**Repository seed:** `mc_lab::tests::semantic::models::explicit_system`.

**Assumption tested:** Establishes the semantic oracle and the initial consumer.

### SUBJECT-GENERATED-LTS-BASELINE-01

**Type:** MODEL and MATERIALIZATION

**Role:** DEVELOPMENT

**Formalism:** Finite unlabelled transition system.

**Representation:** Generated `std::views::iota` range whose witness is an
`int` value.

**Repository seed:** `mc_lab::tests::semantic::models::lazy_system`.

**Use in this case:** Existing exploratory evidence only. It is not the
controlled lazy subject because it lacks transition labelling and does not
provide a strong single-pass or ephemeral-proxy challenge.

### SUBJECT-LAZY-LTS-01

**Type:** MODEL and MATERIALIZATION

**Role:** ADVERSARIAL MATERIALIZATION

**Formalism:** Semantically equivalent to SUBJECT-EXPLICIT-LTS-01 for the
reference source states and labelled transitions.

**Required representation characteristics:**

- outgoing transitions computed from the source state;
- genuinely single-pass input range;
- witnesses yielded by an ephemeral proxy;
- no stable witness address;
- no `size()` or multipass guarantee;
- no persistent transition collection or hidden complete cache.

**Frozen acceptance checks:**

- the outgoing range models `std::ranges::input_range`;
- it does not model `std::ranges::forward_range`,
  `std::ranges::sized_range`, or `std::ranges::borrowed_range`;
- copied iterators share traversal state so advancing one invalidates an earlier
  observation and demonstrates single-pass behavior;
- dereference yields an ephemeral proxy with no persistent occurrence identity
  or stable address;
- label and target are both observable from the same current witness before
  iterator advancement;
- source state, cursor or generator state, and storage for only the current
  ephemeral witness are permitted;
- storing all outgoing label-target pairs, a complete local image, a persistent
  transition collection, or stable descriptors is prohibited;
- compile-time assertions, runtime oracle tests, and source inspection record
  compliance with these checks.

**Assumption tested:** Whether the current semantic surface accidentally
requires stored witnesses or stronger range behavior than the consumer needs.

### CON-SUCCESSORS-01

**Type:** CONSUMER

**Role:** Architectural probe

**Semantic purpose:** Enumerate each outgoing transition once and observe its
label and target. The source state is known from the query.

**Minimum requirements:**

- `outgoing_transitions(system, state)` is a single-pass range;
- `transition_label(system, witness)` works while the witness is valid;
- `target(system, witness)` works while the witness is valid.

**Explicit non-requirements:**

- successor count before iteration;
- multipass iteration;
- random access;
- stable witness address;
- witness validity after iterator advancement;
- persistence after range destruction;
- global exploration;
- mutation.

**Observation output:** The experiment may copy label and target into an owning
normalized result for comparison. That result is experimental evidence and
must not be confused with materialization inside the semantic model.

The frozen semantic value types are `int` for states and `reference_label` for
labels. They are copyable into the evidence result. ARC-001 does not investigate
move-only or noncopyable semantic values.

**Negative applicability case:** A consumer that stores transition-occurrence
identity for a later trace is outside this case.

**Prospective value-category classification:** The frozen consumer binds the
current range observation to the named variable `witness`, so its calls to
`transition_label` and `target` use that observation as an lvalue. If an
ephemeral proxy subject satisfies the declared concepts—which test a value or
proxy reference form—but the unchanged consumer body fails because those CPOs

semantic contract. It supports H-B or contradicts H-A within scope. It is not
an apparatus defect and must be preserved before any consumer, concept, CPO, or

**Frozen negative test:** The controlled subject's compile-time category checks
and runtime use by the unchanged consumer demonstrate that the consumer does
not require `forward_range`, `sized_range`, `borrowed_range`, stable witness
address, or a second pass. Until that test passes, the consumer is described
only as written for single-pass consumption, not as demonstrated compatible.

### Permitted and prohibited adaptations

- model-local member implementations behind current CPOs;
- a local iterator, sentinel, and ephemeral witness/proxy type needed to model
  a C++20 single-pass range;
- compile-time conformance tests and runtime semantic-oracle tests.

Prohibited during the frozen experiment:

- changing consumer logic or the oracle;
- adding representation dispatch;
- adding a persistent transition cache;
- weakening a semantic-integrity guardrail;
- or strengthening the required range category without first preserving and
  recording the discriminator.

### Expected supporting result

Support H-A within scope if:

- CON-SUCCESSORS-01 remains source-unchanged;
- both subjects satisfy the relevant semantic concepts;
- normalized observations are equivalent;
- the lazy subject builds no persistent outgoing-transition collection;
- the consumer requires no more than single-pass enumeration;
- integration requires only the model-local adaptations permitted by the
  freeze.

### Expected contradicting result

Support H-B or contradict H-A if:

- lazy integration must materialize all outgoing transitions;
- the consumer changes;
- stable witness identity is required;
- the concept requires stronger range behavior without a declared semantic
  reason;
- representation-specific branching appears;
- or labels and targets cannot be preserved.

### Inconclusive conditions

- the custom range fails due to an experiment-apparatus defect rather than a
  SemTL requirement;
- compiler behavior cannot be separated from a compiler defect;
- the subject is not genuinely single-pass;
- the explicit and lazy subjects do not implement the same reference
  semantics.

### Semantic-integrity guardrails

- no transition label may be discarded;
- no transition occurrence may be duplicated or omitted;
- normalized labelled targets must agree;
- the adapter must not hide persistent storage;
- witness validity must be documented accurately;
- order is compared only if the reference experiment deliberately assigns it
  meaning; otherwise compare the represented labelled relation.

### Architectural-complexity guardrails

The case may add only:

- one small reusable consumer;
- one genuinely single-pass test model;
- model-local adaptation;
- the C++20 range machinery necessary for the experimental subject.

If support requires several new concepts, multiple dispatch layers, broad
associated-type machinery, persistent caches, or unrelated semantic changes,
stop and assess leverage before proceeding.

### First Failure Preservation Rule

When the controlled experiment produces its first discriminating failure, the
Human Experiment Owner must stop controlled execution before redesign or
repair. Claude may support preservation of:

- the freeze reference and original frozen configuration;
- the exact consumer and subject revisions;
- the command, compiler, standard-library implementation, and environment;
- the complete diagnostic, runtime failure, or semantic mismatch;
- the raw output and smallest faithful reproduction;
- and the observation stated without architectural interpretation.

The failing configuration must remain recoverable in Git or an explicitly
linked evidence artifact. A workaround, consumer change, stronger range
requirement, cache, adapter, or redesign may be attempted only after the
original failure is recorded and must remain distinguishable from it.

Codex may propose an architectural interpretation, and GPT Work may advise on
